What is this map?

This map shows where restrictive immigration policies were adopted across U.S. counties and states between 2004 and 2014.

Federal immigration reforms in 1996, followed by the expansion of immigration enforcement after September 11, 2001, increased the role of state and local governments in immigration enforcement. Over this period, states, counties, and municipalities increasingly adopted their own immigration policies amid stalled federal reform, changing immigrant settlement patterns, and growing political and economic anxieties about immigration; the majority of these policies were restrictive.

This produced a highly uneven patchwork of immigration policy across the country, even among communities within the same state. Policies in this map include Secure Communities participation, mandatory E-Verify, REAL ID implementation, and anti-solicitation ordinances.

Darker shades indicate more restrictive policies. Click a county to see which policy types contribute to its total.

Geographic Level & Restrictive Policies

Geographic level allows users to toggle between County policies and State policies. In State view, all counties within a state share the same color.

Restrictive Policies allows users to toggle between Cumulative and yearly Count data.

Cumulative captures the total number of restrictive policies adopted through the selected year, showing the policy landscape in place at that point in time. Yearly Count captures only the policies adopted in the selected year, highlighting when new policies were enacted.

Exploring the Map

Hover over any county to see its policy count. Click a county to open the breakdown panel, which shows the policy types contributing to that count. The panel always reflects your current Level, Count, and Year selections.

Press ▶ next to the year slider to animate policy adoption from 2004–2014.
